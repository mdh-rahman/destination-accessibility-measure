Methods, limitations and source files

Origin counts weight the access values already calculated at H3 resolution 8. Group selection changes the population summaries and map circles, not route times. The metro and Austin views use existing H3 membership. Destinations outside Austin remain available.

Low access: at or above the selected-area resident-weighted 75th percentile for travel times; at or below the 25th percentile for jobs and acreage. Ties remain together. When the supply threshold is zero, only zero supply is classified low. When the time threshold exceeds the routing limit, non-reached origins are low. Austin uses Austin percentiles; the metro uses metro percentiles. The threshold is fixed across groups within each selected area.

Non-reached travel times remain right-censored. Full-population time means are unavailable when any weighted observations exceed the limit. Conditional means describe only successful routes and can be biased toward better-served locations. Missing values are not zero, and missing access is excluded with its coverage reported. The distributions retain zero opportunity values.

Population: ACS 2020–2024 five-year estimates, allocated using a 2020 Census block population crosswalk. Household and family allocation uses population as a proxy. Disability counts are allocated from tracts. Cells without matched source-block points have unknown population; they are not interpreted as empty. Groups overlap. Accessibility is residential geographic potential, not measured individual travel or participation. Families with children means families with own children under 18. Single-parent families are the ACS no-spouse-present subset; unmarried partners may be present. Long commutes are usual one-way work trips of 60 minutes or more, not a geographic-distance measure; workers at home are excluded from this commute universe.

Transit results inherit supplied GTFS coverage, service-date and routing assumptions; this review does not validate the service calendar or model disability-specific barriers. Auto access for no-vehicle households is hypothetical. Park acreage measures reachable inventory supply, not quality, usable acreage, or acreage per resident. Counts and intervals are estimates, not precise individual outcomes.
